Compact, Low-Cost Kelvin Probe

A capacitive Kelvin-probe system for measuring contact potential difference (CPD) and tracking changes in surface work function, built around accessible components and programmable electronics.

Macroscopic work-function measurements made practical for teaching, student projects, and low-cost surface-science instrumentation.

How it works

A small gold probe is periodically vibrated above a stationary sample, changing the capacitance across the probe-sample gap. When their work functions differ, this capacitance modulation produces a small AC current.

A compensation voltage is swept to null the current. The nullification point gives the CPD, and therefore the work-function difference between the two materials.

Digital lock-in detection

A piezoelectric buzzer plate supplies the mechanical vibration. Its reference waveform and the Kelvin-probe signal are processed on a Teensy 4.1 microcontroller using in-phase and quadrature detection, digital mixing, and low-pass filtering.

The resulting amplitude and phase reveal the phase reversal that identifies the CPD during a compensation-voltage sweep.

Instrument highlights

The platform combines a simple capacitive probe with flexible, real-time digital signal processing.

Accessible architecture

Commercial piezoelectric buzzer actuator, capacitive probe, transimpedance amplifier, and XYZ positioning for alignment.

Standalone operation

A self-resonant driver sustains motion near the buzzer’s mechanical resonance without an external function generator.

Adaptive processing

Reference-signal frequency estimation, programmable filtering, and phase-sensitive detection are implemented in firmware.

Measurement resolution

Demonstrated CPD resolution is approximately 10 mV with external function-generator excitation and approximately 100 mV with the present self-resonant driver.

Learning platform

The instrument connects electronics, digital signal processing, physics, instrumentation, programming, and surface science in one hands-on experiment.

What it can study

The capacitive Kelvin probe is suited to macroscopic CPD and work-function measurements, including comparisons between materials and changes caused by surface modification, adsorption, or oxidation.

Because its processing pipeline is programmable, it is also a useful starting point for developing custom frequency tracking, filtering, and automated measurement strategies.

Complementary to KPFM

Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) integrates Kelvin-probe measurements with atomic-force microscopy to map surface potential at the nanoscale. This instrument instead prioritizes a simple, accessible route to CPD measurement and experimental learning.
